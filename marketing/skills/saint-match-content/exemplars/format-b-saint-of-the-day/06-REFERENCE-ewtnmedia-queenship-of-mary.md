## What it disproves: the invocation is table stakes

An earlier reading of this folder held that **ending on the invocation** was the
key Format B differentiator. The two strong posts end there; the worst post (`04`)
does not; the inference was easy and it was wrong.

**This post closes devotionally.** "Mother of Mercy, our life, our sweetness, and
our hope!" is the Salve Regina, sitting exactly in the invocation's slot and doing
its liturgical job. And it lands **~2.5× below** Clare and Monica on likes and
**~6× below** on saves.

So the rule is restated, and this is the version to carry:

> **Ending on the invocation is necessary but not sufficient. It is table
> stakes.** What separates a strong Format B post is that it **gives the reader a
> reason to stop**: a **numbered promise with a named audience** (variant B-2,
> `03`), or a **hinge carrying the saint's life into what the brand does**
> (variant B-1, `01`). A post with neither is inert, however correctly it ends.

| Post | Likes | Saves | Hook or hinge? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `03` St Monica (B-2) | 7,194 | 1,306 | numbered promise + named audience |
| `01` St Clare (B-1) | 7,131 | 882 | hinge word — *broadcast* |
| `06` **this post** | 2,871 | 148 | **neither** |
| `04` St Joseph Calasanz | 996 | 69 | **neither**, and no invocation |

Read that table only with the README's caveats attached. It is four posts, and
subject salience is uncontrolled.

**A grading consequence:** `SCORER.md` W3 still STOPs a post that does not end on
the invocation, and it still does so on form — "Mother of Mercy, our life, our
sweetness, and our hope!" is devout and is **not** the required form, so this
caption would itself STOP at W3 under Saint Match's floor. But a **W3 PASS is not
evidence a post works.** That is W2, or W6 with W7.

## The opening-line tell

**"August 22 is the feast day of…"** — a calendar announcement, the same opener as
`04`. Both posts that open this way are encyclopedic and both are the folder's
two underperformers; both strong posts open on the reader or straight into the
situation.

**Treat "opens by announcing the date or the feast" as an anti-pattern marker**
that co-occurs with encyclopedia register (`best_skill.md` §5.7, `SCORER.md`
B1-B). It is the cheapest available diagnostic: if the first six words name a
date, read the rest for register before anything else.

The middle sentence — "We ask her to intercede for us" — is the other half of the
inertness. It states the devotional act rather than performing anything, which is
the same class of move as `05`'s stated bridge.

## What it gets RIGHT: the image quote is properly attributed

Recorded as a **positive** in an otherwise weak post, and it is a genuine one.

The image quote does **not float free**. It carries a **specific, documented
source**: *"Our Lady to Saint Faustina"* — i.e. Faustina Kowalska's *Diary*,
a named text by a named writer, which is checkable.

This is the correct **shape** of attribution, and it is exactly what `04`'s image
lacks: there, "Those who instruct many in justice will shine as stars for all
eternity" is attributed to Joseph Calasanz when it is **Daniel 12:3**, which
Calasanz himself quoted *and explicitly marked as quoted* — an attribution that
names a person but points at nothing checkable. That case has since been
verified and closed; see `04` and Case 1 of `ATTRIBUTION-CASES.md`.

**The distinction worth carrying:**

| | `06` (this post) | `04` (Calasanz) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Named source | Yes — Our Lady to St Faustina (the *Diary*) | Yes — "St Joseph Calasanz" |
| **Points at a checkable text** | **Yes** — a specific work by a named writer | **No** — no work, no locus |
| Verified here? | **No** | **Yes — and it failed.** Daniel 12:3, quoted by the saint under an explicit "As Scripture says" |
| Status | **Properly-formed attribution** | **LOOSE-TO-WRONG.** Real words, real saint, dropped citation frame |

**Do not read this as verification.** The Faustina citation is **properly formed,
not confirmed.** Under `best_skill.md` §7.2 and §7.3 and `SCORER.md` **T3**, a
Saint Match pack quoting this line would still need the *Diary* paragraph number
in the dossier and the wording checked against the text before it could be used;
"attributed to a named source" is the shape a citation must have, not a
substitute for having made the check. A well-formed citation that turns out to be
wrong is still a T3 STOP.

## Engagement caveat specific to this post

**2,871 likes against `04`'s 996, on the same account, is not 3× the craft.**
Mary is incomparably better known than Joseph Calasanz. A large part of that gap
is **subject salience**, not writing.

That is precisely why the SkillOpt loop must normalise engagement against a
saint-popularity baseline before comparing posts (`SCORER.md` §9.5). This
specimen is what turns that from a design assumption into an observation in real
data: two posts, same account, same register, same anti-pattern opener, ~3×
apart on likes, with subject fame as the obvious available explanation.
