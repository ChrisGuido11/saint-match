## Caption — the load-bearing line

```
As the patron saint of the Augustine Institute, St. Augustine holds a special
place in our mission.
```

The post is otherwise correct B-1 shape and **ends on the invocation**.

---

## Why it fails: the bridge is stated, not performed

This is the subtlest failure in the folder, and the most instructive, because
**almost every structural check passes**:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Third person | ✓ |
| Length in band | ✓ |
| Ends on the invocation (W3) | ✓ |
| No pitch, no hashtag wall, no emoji (W4) | ✓ |
| **A hinge (W2)** | **✗ — and this is the whole post** |

The sentence **announces a relationship instead of performing one.** "Holds a
special place in our mission" is a claim *about* a connection; it is not the
connection doing anything. Nothing from Augustine's life — not the *Confessions*,
not the years of resisting, not the garden, not Monica — is carried over into
what the organisation does. The saint's name is on the door and that is the
entire link.

**The saint is a mascot, not a hinge.** A mascot is a name borrowed for
identity. A hinge is a specific thing the saint carried, reappearing as a
specific thing the brand does. Compare `01`: Clare saw Mass "broadcast" on a
wall; EWTN broadcasts Mass. One word, load-bearing on both sides, never
explained. Here there is no word doing double duty at all.

**Apply the swap test** (`SCORER.md` W2, `best_skill.md` §6): substitute any
other saint. "As the patron saint of the [X] Institute, St [N] holds a special
place in our mission" reads exactly as well. The sentence is not anchored to
Augustine's life; it is anchored to the org chart. That is the definition of a
generic bridge.

**Why it reads well anyway.** The register is warm, the shape is right, the
ending is right. This is precisely the draft that gets waved through by a grader
running down a checklist, and it is why `SCORER.md` W2 makes **inability to name
the hinge the STOP itself** rather than a prompt to look harder. Run W2 on this
caption honestly and you cannot name a hinge word — so it STOPs, and the checklist
being otherwise green is not a mitigation.

## The Saint Match version of this exact mistake

Saint Match has the same temptation in a sharper form, because the app really
does have a saint-matching function and it is very easy to *say* so:

- ✗ *"St Augustine spent years resisting. Saint Match helps you find the saint who
  understands your struggle."* — stated. An explained hinge is a dead hinge
  (`best_skill.md` §5.7).
- ✓ *"Augustine spent years knowing what he should do and not doing it. Some
  people are still in that gap. St Augustine, pray for us."* — performed. The app
  is never named; the reader completes the connection.

The difference is not politeness or length. It is whether the reader is told
about the match or shown it happening.
