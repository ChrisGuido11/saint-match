# 03 — St Francis of Assisi — STOP (invented / misattributed quote)

The failure this example demonstrates is the most common one and the hardest to
see, because the offending sentence is *good*. It fits the saint, it fits the
theme, it scans, and a devout reader will nod at it. It is also not his.

```
draft_id       2026-10-04-francis-of-assisi
skill_version  best_skill.md @ v0-seed
format         carousel-4x5
saint          St Francis of Assisi
feast_date     4 October (Memorial, General Roman Calendar)
topic          vocation / the thing you have been avoiding
```

**Excluded, and why**

- **D5**, the "use words" line, is listed in the dossier *specifically as a
  misattribution* so that a writer reaching for it finds it flagged. It is not
  usable copy.
- The wolf of Gubbio, the sermon to the birds, and the meeting with the Sultan
  as usually told. LEGEND or heavily embroidered; none is needed here.

### Description caption

```
St Francis of Assisi, founder of the Friars Minor

He told his brothers to preach the Gospel at all times and to use words only when necessary, and he meant it as a rebuke to men who talk.

Before any of that he was a cloth merchant's son who could not stand the sight of a leper. He says so himself, at the end of his life, in the one document he dictated as a last word: it seemed too bitter to me to see lepers. Then, he writes, God led him among them and he showed them mercy, and what had been bitter turned to sweetness of soul and body. And afterwards I delayed a little and left the world.

The conversion is not the sweetness. The conversion is the going, before there was any sweetness in it.

St Francis of Assisi, deacon and founder of the Friars Minor, pray for us.
```

## The corrected pack

Only the two flagged elements change. Everything else stands.

**Slide 1 — hook (replacement)**

> "Let all the brothers
> preach by their works."

*Earlier Rule of the Friars Minor, 1221*

**Description caption (replacement)**

```
St Francis of Assisi, founder of the Friars Minor

His own Rule told the brothers to preach by their works. He also preached out loud, often, sometimes several times a day, and the order he left behind was built around doing exactly that. The two were never in competition.

Before any of it he was a cloth merchant's son who could not stand the sight of a leper. He says so himself, at the end of his life, in the one document he dictated as a last word: it seemed too bitter to me to see lepers. Then, he writes, God led him among them and he showed them mercy, and what had been bitter turned to sweetness of soul and body. And afterwards I delayed a little and left the world.

The conversion is not the sweetness. The conversion is the going, before there was any sweetness in it.

St Francis of Assisi, deacon and founder of the Friars Minor, pray for us.
```

The corrected version is also a better post. It gets a real quotation, it gets
the actual historical Francis instead of the quietist one the misquotation
invents, and it gets an extra beat — "The two were never in competition" — that
the false version could not have reached. This is worth noticing: the truth gate
is not only a tax. It frequently produces the sharper draft, because a real
source has detail in it and an invented one has only sentiment.
