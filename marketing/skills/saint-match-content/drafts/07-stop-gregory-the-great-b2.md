## 2. Topic + theme bridge *(internal)*

**Topic:** vocation — being in work you did not choose.

**Virtue:** fidelity in an office not sought. Gregory left the top civil job in Rome for
a monastery (D3, D4), was elected pope against his stated wish (D10), wrote in his own
hand that he had "lost the deep joys of my quiet" (D11), and then did the work — the
title, the *Pastoral Rule*, the mission to Britain — for fourteen years.

**Micro-action (5–15 min):** *Take the one task on today's list that was handed to you
rather than chosen by you. Do it first, and do it as carefully as the work you picked.*

**Swap test:** substitute **St Ambrose** — acclaimed bishop of Milan against his will —
and the bridge sentence still reads perfectly. **FAILS.** Recorded honestly; see the
verdict.

**Swap test, with a named substitute: St Ambrose.** "4 things St Ambrose can teach
anyone doing work they never chose" reads perfectly well. Ambrose was acclaimed bishop
of Milan while still a catechumen and is the standard case of the office nobody sought.
Substitute St Augustine of Hippo — pressed into ordination at Hippo — and it also holds.
**The hinge survives the swap. It is therefore generic, and W2 STOPs.**

This is recorded rather than argued away. §5.7 is explicit that "faith", "courage" and
"perseverance" survive the swap and are not hinges; *"the work you did not choose"* is
in that class, one notch more specific and still not anchored to **this** life.

## SF — Spec findings: where `best_skill.md` and `SCORER.md` break

Recorded while building and grading two real packs. Ordered roughly by how much damage
each does. **SF-2 is the one that matters.**

### SF-1 — W5's "exactly one fact" never defines a fact *(underspecified → STOPs correct posts)*

W5 (B-1) passes on "one such fact… doing all the anchoring work" and STOPs on "more than
one". Nothing in either document says whether the unit is a sentence, a claim, a dossier
line, or an independently falsifiable proposition. Pack 06's fact — *"the Pastoral Rule
spends thirty-four chapters telling one kind of listener from another — the impatient
from the patient, the too silent from the ones who talk too much"* — is one text, one
dossier line, one sentence, and three separately checkable propositions. It was graded as
one. A different grader counts three and STOPs.

Worse, §1's fail-closed default ("grader cannot determine whether a check passes →
STOP") means the ambiguity *by itself* forces the STOP. As written, **W5 STOPs any B-1
caption whose one fact has internal structure**, which is nearly every fact worth
building a post on. The Raymond specimen only escapes because "they padlocked his mouth
shut" is atomic.

**Fix:** define the unit as **one dossier line**, in W5 and in §4.4.1, and say that
detail internal to that line is not a second fact.

### SF-2 — W2 and W7 are close to mutually unsatisfiable on variant B-2 *(impossible to satisfy)*

This is the finding this pack exists to report.

- W2 requires the hinge to **fail the swap test** — substitute another saint and it must
  collapse.
- On B-2, W2's own table **defines** the app side of the hinge to be the **audience line
  on the hook slide**.
- W7 requires that audience line to be **one of twelve generic struggle categories** from
  `best_skill.md` §1, "or a plain paraphrase of one".

A generic category survives a saint swap almost by construction — genericity is what
makes it a category that many readers can self-select into. So the app side of every
B-2 hinge is under pressure to be exactly the kind of phrase W2 calls not-a-hinge.

This pack STOPs on precisely that. "Anyone doing work they never chose" is a clean W7
pass — vocation, phrased as a thing carried rather than a category of person — and it
fails W2 against St Ambrose, the standard reluctant-bishop case, and again against St
Augustine of Hippo.

The only escape found was to put a **word out of the saint's own mouth** into the
audience line: "anyone who cannot get back to **the quiet**", from *Registrum
Epistolarum* I.5. That clears W2 — and it is at best a loose paraphrase of "distraction",
so it now risks a W7 STOP. **I could not construct a version of this post that is known
to pass both checks.**

The single B-2 specimen gives no help: EWTN's "every Catholic parent" is a demographic
and fails W7 by construction, so the format's only worked example has never been tested
against W2's swap at all. **B-2's two hardest checks have never been satisfied
simultaneously by anything, including its own specimen.**

**Fix — pick one:**
- **(a)** Scope W2's swap test on B-2 to the **saint side only** (the numbered slides).
  That is where the anchoring genuinely lives on this variant, and it is consistent with
  W9's finding that the slides carry the post.
- **(b)** Let W7 accept a struggle phrased in the **saint's own vocabulary**, add
  "the quiet" as the worked example, and say explicitly that the twelve-topic list
  governs the internal `topic` field rather than the surface wording of the audience line.

Either would work. Doing neither leaves B-2 as a format whose spec cannot be met.

### SF-3 — the twelve struggle topics are a closed list, and they are the app's topics, not the world's *(too narrow)*

Gregory's documented struggle — a man who wanted a cell and got the largest
administrative job in the Latin West, and said so in writing — has no clean entry.
"vocation" is nearest, and W7 itself flags vocation as the one that runs closest to a
demographic. **Fix:** declare the list open-ended ("or another struggle the dossier
evidences, named in the topic field"), or lengthen it.

### SF-4 — the same closed list governs the `topic` header field, and forces stretches

Pack 06's topic is recorded as **loneliness** for a post about being addressed only in
general. That is a stretch and it is forced by the list, not by the material. The honest
topic is something like "generic advice / not being known", which does not exist.

### SF-5 — B-2 has no slide-count floor, and W6 makes one necessary *(underspecified)*

§4.4.1 and §3B.0 both refuse to prescribe a total, correctly — the one specimen is
unrecorded past its hook slide. But W6 makes the *number* the contract and nothing stops
a B-2 post promising **2**. A promise of two is a B-1 with extra steps, and W9 would
still pass because the two slides really would carry the teaching. **Fix:** a floor of
**three** teaching slides, marked explicitly as a stipulation rather than as evidence.

### SF-6 — the 5–15 word slide limit is unbudgeted for citations, and on a quotation slide it is unsatisfiable *(impossible to satisfy)*

§4.4.1 keeps 5–15 words per slide on Format B. T3 STOPs any quotation "whose citation is
absent, vague… or unverifiable". Slide 3 here quotes Gregory in 13 words including its
lead-in; the shortest honest attribution — "Gregory, *Letter to Theoctista*" — is 4
more, taking it to 17. Pack 06 hit the same wall from the other side: its overlay was
drafted with "St Gregory the Great, Pastoral Rule III" as the attribution line, which
took the block to **16 words and over the limit**, and the attribution was cut to
"Gregory, Pastoral Rule III" purely to fit. **The citation was shortened by the word
limit.** Nothing anywhere says whether an attribution counts toward that limit.

§7.2's sentence — "the citation goes in the source notes even when it does not fit on the
slide" — is the only thing keeping this passable, and no check mentions it, so a grader
reading T3 against the slide sees an unattributed quotation.

**Fix:** state in §4 that attribution lines are excluded from the 5–15 count, and add to
T3 that a slide-level citation may live in the source notes with a pointer.

### SF-7 — §8.0's "name a reference artwork" collides with I2 whenever the canonical type is wrong *(self-contradictory)*

§8.0: "Include a named reference artwork where one exists." For Gregory the canonical
type — Ribera's *Saint Gregory the Great* and effectively the whole standard — shows
**tiara and pontificals**, which I2 forbids and D25 says outright is "despite his actual
habit of dress". Naming a reference artwork here imports the exact error I2 exists to
prevent. Both packs withhold one and say why. **Fix:** "…unless the canonical type
carries an attribute I2 excludes, in which case name none and record the reason."

### SF-8 — §8.0's slide template requires the `@saintmatchapp` handle on-image; §5.4 forbids the app in any on-image text *(self-contradictory)*

§8.0's brand-background journey-slide spec puts the handle at 8% from top. §5.4 says
"The app is never named in the body or in any on-image text". §5.4 sits under Format A
and W4 bans pitching rather than naming, so the handle is *probably* fine on Format B —
but "probably", plus §1's fail-closed default, means a grader must STOP. Both packs omit
it. **Fix:** one sentence saying whether the handle is permitted on Format B slides.

### SF-9 — `draft_id` collides when two packs share a saint and a day *(wrong, and it is the analytics join key)*

§3.1 fixes the format as `<ISO date>-<saint-slug>`. This task produced two packs for one
saint on one day and they cannot both be `2026-09-03-gregory-the-great`. Both packs here
append `-b1` / `-b2`, which is off-contract. Since `draft_id` is the join key for
externally collected engagement data, a collision silently merges two posts' metrics.
**Fix:** put the variant in the id, or add a documented disambiguator suffix.

### SF-10 — `best_skill.md` §3.1 and `SCORER.md` §8 disagree about `format_variant` on Format A

§3.1: a Format A pack carrying a variant value is a STOP. §8's output template prints
`format_variant: … | — (Format A)`. A grader filling the template has to know that the
em dash is a placeholder and not a value. Cosmetic, but it is the kind of thing an LLM
judge copies literally into a pack header.

### SF-11 — §1 demands a bespoke `N/A` justification for T4 on every Format B pack, forever

The single exception to bespoke justification is format/variant mismatch. T4 on Format B
is neither: Format B structurally has no scripture block (3B), so T4 is `N/A` on
essentially every Format B pack for the same reason every time, and the grader must
write a fresh sentence saying so. **Fix:** add "N/A — no scripture cited in the pack" to
the list of sufficient justifications.

### SF-12 — B1-B's register checks are written about the caption, and on B-2 the caption is twenty words *(checkable in principle, unreachable in practice)*

Encyclopedia register is named as "the primary Format B anti-pattern" and B1-B is where
it is caught. B1-B describes it as a property of "a caption". On B-2 the caption is a
question and an invocation; the teaching — and therefore all the register risk — is on
the slides. **W4 explicitly extends to slide overlays on B-2; B1 does not say.** So the
primary anti-pattern of the format is unchecked on the only surface where a B-2 post can
commit it.

This pack's slides 2, 4 and 5 are exactly the clipped biographical statements that read
encyclopedic in bulk. They were graded PASS on substance — they are beats, not a run of
appositives — but the check as written could not have reached them either way.
**Fix:** extend B1-B to slide overlays on B-2, in the same words W4 uses.

### SF-13 — I4 demands the type zone "as a vertical percentage range", but §8.1's own example is horizontal *(self-contradictory)*

I4's evidence line: "the type zone as a vertical percentage range". §8.1's second worked
example: "the hook figure in the left 40% with the right 60% bright and empty" — a
**side** band, which no vertical range can describe. This pack's hook slide uses a side
band and supplies both axes. **Fix:** "as a percentage range on the axis that defines the
zone, with the extent on the other axis given."

### SF-14 — the 5–15 word slide limit makes §4.3's Collect slide impossible *(impossible to satisfy)*

§4.3 keeps the Raymond liturgical slide roles — hook / maxim / named author / **Collect**
/ invocation — available as a variant, and says taking the variant "does not override…
the 5–15 word limit". The Collect for St Gregory (D27) is 55 words and §7.5 forbids
paraphrasing liturgical text. **The permitted variant contains a slide that can never be
rendered legally.** Neither pack uses it; the contradiction stands regardless. **Fix:**
exempt liturgical text from the word limit, or withdraw the Collect slide from the
permitted variant.

### SF-15 — W8's 8–25 word band is decoration *(soft, as the document already admits)*

This caption is 20 words. 9 would have passed, 24 would have passed. The band constrained
nothing. What actually shapes a B-2 caption is W8's "exactly two elements and nothing
else", which is a shape rule. The band is n=1 around a 14-word specimen and is currently
performing measurement theatre. **Fix:** keep recording counts, stop treating the band as
a check until n ≥ 4, and say so in W8 the way §9.5 says it for the voice score.

### SF-16 — I4 clause 3 cannot be run by a grader at gate time *(checkable in principle, not in practice)*

"A mid-tone or otherwise clean region is specified behind the type, **sufficient for the
type to clear 3.5:1 against it**". Contrast ratio is a property of the rendered image. At
gate time there is no image — the pack contains a prompt. Both packs here assert 3.5:1
and nobody has measured anything; the assertion is unfalsifiable at the point the check
runs. **Fix:** split the clause. A prompt-time part ("a contrast provision is specified,
with named hex values on both sides") and a post-generation part ("measured on the
rendered file before publication"), and say which one the gate runs.

### SF-17 — nothing requires the dossier to record whether the feast is actually celebrated that year *(missing)*

T5 tests "feast date, liturgical rank" against the dossier. Nothing requires the dossier
to carry the **supersession** check — whether the memorial survives on the day, or is
displaced by a Sunday, a solemnity or a feast. `drafts/README.md` records this informally
for St Francis (4 October 2026 is a Sunday) and draft 03 keeps it as a dossier line, but
it is a convention, not a requirement. It is the one calendar fact that changes year to
year, and it is the one a model will get wrong most confidently. **Fix:** make it a
required dossier row and a named clause in T5.

### SF-18 — the §4.4.0 engagement table now lives in three places

`exemplars/format-b-saint-of-the-day/README.md` each restate the same four rows with
their own caveats attached. Three places to update, three places to drift, and the
caveats are already worded differently in each. Not a correctness bug today. It will be
one. **Fix:** one canonical copy in the exemplars README; the other two cite it.
