**Excluded, and why**

- **The "two crowns" story** — that as a child he was offered a white crown and a red crown by the Virgin. It comes from his mother's recollection recounted after his death. TRADITIONAL at best. Not used, not depicted, not alluded to.
- **His popular patronages** (prisoners, addicts, journalists, families). Customary rather than decreed, and this dossier carries no basis for them, so the invocation uses the canonisation designation at D14 instead.
- **Any quotation from his own writings.** He left plenty (D18) but none is in this dossier with a locus, so none is used. Aptness is not a source.
- **The prisoner escape narrative details** — the escapee's name and fate are given inconsistently across sources and are not needed. Omitted rather than hedged.

## Topic + theme bridge *(internal)*

**Topic:** fear — specifically the fear of a cost that is not yours and that you could simply let fall on someone else.

**Virtue:** substitution. Not generic self-sacrifice but the concrete, unfeeling, procedural act of putting your name where another name was (D8, D9). Kolbe's was not a rush of feeling; it was a step out of a line at a roll call, followed by two weeks of dying.

**Micro-action (5–15 min):** *Do one piece of work today that had been left to someone else, and let it be found done without saying it was you.*

**Swap test:** substitute any other martyr and the sentence breaks — the bridge depends on a named man being taken off a list and another man going in his place, which is specific to D7–D9.

## Description caption

```
St Maximilian Kolbe, priest of the Order of Friars Minor Conventual

In late July 1941 a prisoner escaped from Auschwitz. The camp answered the way it answered everything: ten men from the same block, picked out at roll call, to be shut in an underground cell and starved. One of the ten, Franciszek Gajowniczek, said aloud that he had a wife and children. Kolbe stepped out of the line and asked to go in his place. The deputy commandant accepted the exchange. He was under no obligation to.

Kolbe was forty-seven and had spent the previous decade running a printing house. The block interpreter later testified that in the cell he led the others in prayer, and that the corridors of the bunker were full of it. The others died over about two weeks. Kolbe did not, so on 14 August the guards ended it with an injection of carbolic acid.

We tend to imagine sacrifice as a moment of feeling. This one was a procedure—a line, a count, one name entered in place of another—and then a fortnight in a cell while it took effect.

St Maximilian Kolbe, priest and martyr of charity, pray for us.
```
