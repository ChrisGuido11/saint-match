## Dossier

Nearly everything known about Monica comes from one source: her son. Augustine's
*Confessions* is a primary text by a named participant, written within about a
decade of her death. That makes it unusually strong evidence and unusually
partial evidence at the same time, and the pack says so.

**Excluded, and why**

- **That the bishop of D5 was Ambrose.** He was not, and *Confessions* III.12
  does not name him — the episode happens in Africa, years before Milan. This is
  one of the most common errors made about Monica and the slide attribution is
  written specifically to block it.
- **The black leather belt** said to have been given to Monica by the Virgin
  Mary, the basis of the Augustinian cincture devotion. LEGEND, and much later.
  Not mentioned, not depicted.
- **"Seventeen years of prayer."** A widely repeated figure with no basis in the
  dossier. The pack uses Augustine's own ages instead — nineteen to thirty-two —
  because those are in the text.
- **Any quotation attributed to Monica beyond D11 and D12**, and any
  reconstruction of what she prayed.

## Topic + theme bridge *(internal)*

**Topic:** waiting — praying for a specific person over years with no visible movement.

**Virtue:** persistence in intercession, held without a guarantee. Monica's case
is unusually clean because the timeline is documented from both ends: he was
nineteen when he went (D4), thirty-two when he was baptised (D8), and she died
months later (D10).

**Micro-action (5–15 min):** *Pray for one person you have quietly stopped
expecting anything from. By name, for five minutes. Then put it in tomorrow's
calendar.*

**Swap test:** substituting almost any other saint breaks it — the bridge rests
on a named person prayed for across a documented thirteen-year gap that closed
only at the very end of the pray-er's life.

**Excluded from the image, and why**

- **A nun's habit, wimple, veil-and-scapular, or an Augustinian black habit.**
  Monica was never a religious, and the Augustinian order did not exist in her
  lifetime. She is nonetheless painted this way constantly. This is the same
  class of error as the Raymond Nonnatus cardinalate: later art repeating a
  mistake is not evidence for it.
- **The Augustinian cincture.** Excluded per the dossier.
- **A rosary, a book, Gothic or Romanesque architecture.** No dossier support;
  all anachronistic to 387.
- **Weeping.** She is called a woman of tears in the liturgy (D15), but a
  generator given "weeping" will produce a sentimental face. The prompt asks for
  dry eyes and an unsoftened expression instead, which is the register.

## Description caption

```
St Monica, mother of Augustine of Hippo

Almost everything we know about her was written by the son she spent her life worrying about. He is not a neutral witness and does not pretend to be.

She married a pagan and buried him. Her son took up with the Manichees at nineteen and stayed. She asked a bishop to argue him out of it; the bishop refused, and told her only that it was not possible that the son of these tears should perish. When Augustine sailed for Rome he lied to her about it and left at night while she prayed on the shore. She followed him anyway, all the way to Milan.

He was baptised there at Easter, in his thirty-third year. Monica died a few months later at Ostia, waiting for the boat home, and asked to be remembered at the altar and nothing else.

Thirteen years is a long time to be told that a prayer is working while nothing visibly happens. She got her answer with months to spare, and nobody had promised her she would.

St Monica, patron saint of mothers and of difficult marriages, pray for us.
```
