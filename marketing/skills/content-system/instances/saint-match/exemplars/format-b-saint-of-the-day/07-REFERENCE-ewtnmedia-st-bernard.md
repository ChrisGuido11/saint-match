## Finding 1 — "promotion of the Memorare" is MISATTRIBUTED

Bernard of Clairvaux did not write, compose or promote the **Memorare**.

- **The prayer is fifteenth century** — roughly **300 years after Bernard**
  (d. 1153). It first appears as part of the longer prayer *Ad sanctitatis tuae
  pedes, dulcissima Virgo Maria*, of which the Memorare is the opening section
  later detached and prayed on its own.
- **It was popularised by a different Bernard: Fr Claude Bernard
  (1588–1641)**, a French priest who worked among prisoners and the condemned and
  printed and distributed **200,000+ leaflets** carrying the prayer, in several
  languages. He credited his own recovery from illness to it.
- **The name is the whole mechanism.** A prayer spread at scale by *Bernard*
  attaches, over time, to the far more famous *Bernard*. Nobody decided this; the
  attribution is an artefact of two men sharing a name and one of them being a
  Doctor of the Church.
- **Source:** Herbert Thurston SJ, *Familiar Prayers: Their Origin and History*
  (1953), which is the standard treatment and traces the migration directly.
- **The modern form was indulgenced by Pius IX on 11 December 1846** — a useful
  hard date, and worth noting that an official act attaches to the *prayer*, not
  to any claim about Bernard's authorship.

**Why this one matters more than an ordinary bad quote:** the caption does not
put words in Bernard's mouth. It makes a **biographical claim about what he is
known for** — a softer-looking sentence that no quotation check would catch,
because there is no quotation in it. Under `SCORER.md` this fails **T1** (no
dossier line supports it) and **T5** (a biographical claim that is not in the
record), not T3. **Attribution failures are not confined to quoted strings**, and
a gate that only inspects text inside quotation marks will pass this.

## Finding 2 — the image quote IS genuine, and here is the citation

> "In dangers, in doubts, in difficulties, think of Mary, call upon Mary…"

**This one is real.** It is from Bernard's *Homiliae super Missus est* —

- **Hom. II.17**, **PL 183:70–71**;
- quoted by **Pius XII, *Doctor Mellifluus* (1953) §31**, which is the
  **recommended citation** for practical use: it is an official papal act,
  freely available, and it reproduces the passage in a stable, checkable place.

So the post is carrying, side by side, a **misattributed biographical claim** and
a **properly sourced quotation**. That combination is the useful thing about it.
A single post is not "reliable" or "unreliable"; each claim is graded
individually, which is what the dossier convention exists to force.

## Finding 3 — the engine, and what this post disproves

**This post opens by announcing the calendar and performs well.**

| Post | Likes | Saves | Opens on the calendar? | Engine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `03` Monica (B-2) | 7,194 | 1,306 | no | numbered promise + named audience |
| `01` Clare (B-1) | 7,131 | 882 | no | hinge word — *broadcast* |
| **`07` Bernard** | **5,324** | **552** | **yes** | **a genuinely quotable quote** |
| `06` Queenship | 2,871 | 148 | yes | **none** |
| `04` Calasanz | 996 | 69 | yes | **none** |

The earlier reading of this folder was that "opens by announcing the date or the
feast" is **the cheapest available tell** for an inert post, because the two
posts that opened that way were the two underperformers. **`07` refutes that as a
predictor.** It opens the same way and lands second on saves in the whole set.

**The correction, and it is a correction of one claim only:**

> The opening formula is a **register** marker, not a **performance** predictor.
> What predicts performance is whether the post has an **engine** — something
> that gives the reader a reason to stop. The two things co-occurred in `04` and
> `06` by coincidence of a small sample, and the co-occurrence was read as a
> mechanism.

**What survives:** the calendar-announcement opener stays **banned for Saint
Match** (`best_skill.md` §5.7, `SCORER.md` B1-B) on register grounds — it opens
on the calendar rather than on the reader or the fact, and it remains a cheap
prompt to reread a draft for encyclopedia register. **What does not survive:**
citing it as evidence that a post will underperform, or treating its absence as
evidence that a post has an engine.

### The engine list, which is what to carry forward

Reading `01`, `03` and `07` together, an engine is one of:

- **(a) A caption hinge** — a word or concept carried from the saint's life into
  what the brand does, performed and never explained. `01` Clare, *broadcast*.
  `SCORER.md` W2.
- **(b) A hook with a named audience** — a numbered promise plus an explicit
  audience on the hook slide. `03` Monica. `SCORER.md` W6 + W7.
- **(c) A genuinely quotable quote** — a line the reader would screenshot,
  share or send to someone. **`07` is the specimen for this one**, and it is why
  the post survives its own opener: the engine is on the image, not in the
  caption.

A post with none of the three is inert however correctly it ends (`06`), and
ending on the invocation is table stakes rather than an engine.

### Two cautions on engine (c), before anyone reaches for it

1. **Engine (c) is not a floor check and is not being made one.** W2, W6 and W7
   are testable; "genuinely quotable" is not, and writing a check that pretends
   otherwise would put a taste judgement inside the gate, which `SCORER.md` §0.1
   forbids. Record it as an observed engine type and let the voice score notice
   it qualitatively.
2. **For Saint Match, engine (c) is the most dangerous of the three**, because
   the pull of a quotable line is exactly the pressure that produces
   misattribution — and **this very post demonstrates both halves of that at
   once**: its quotable line is genuine and citable, and the sentence next to it
   is a misattribution. A quote reaches an image only with a work-and-locus
   citation in the dossier (`best_skill.md` §7, `SCORER.md` T3). No exceptions,
   and least of all for the ones that would perform best.

## Engagement caveat

Same account as `01`, `04` and `06`, so follower count is substantially
controlled — but **subject salience is not**. Bernard of Clairvaux is a Doctor of
the Church and far better known than Joseph Calasanz, and the post is Marian in
subject, which the folder already has reason to think travels. Read this row with
the README's "How to read the engagement table" caveats attached, as with every
other row.

The claim this row is being asked to support is narrow and it holds: **a post
with a calendar opener can perform, so the opener is not the predictor.** That is
a refutation of a heuristic, which needs only one counter-example, and it does
not depend on the size of the gap.
